What is QTA?

A training, survey and assessment company

Most of our work is health and safety, and all of it leans on the local rules. The law in Belfast is not worded the same as the law in Birmingham or Dublin, so every course and every report names the regulation that applies where you work. Use the switch at the top of this page to see yours.

We have worked with our accreditation body, UK PAAS, since the day QTA opened, developing our own courses and standards with them. The result for you is fuller courses and certificates that have been checked by someone other than us.

Risk assessment

A walk-through of how you actually work: slips and trips, electrics, sharps, lone working, manual handling, visitors and children on the premises.

Fire risk assessment

Sources of ignition, escape routes, alarms, extinguishers, emergency lighting, who is at risk and what to fix first. Written up as a record you can hand to an inspector.

COSHH assessment

Hair colour and bleach, acrylic liquid and dust, disinfectants, inks, cleaning chemicals. We list what you hold, how people are exposed and the controls you need.

Detailed fire surveys

For business and industry: a deeper survey of the building, its compartmentation and its fire precautions, beyond a standard assessment.

Consent to discharge drainage surveys

QTA is one of the first in the country to provide consent to discharge surveys for the latest septic tanks and treatment systems.

How to read a PPE label

Whoever you buy from, these are the standards to look for on workwear of this kind. Ask any supplier, including us, which ones each garment is certified to.

StandardWhat it coversWhy it matters
EN 343Protection against rainRates water penetration and breathability in classes. Higher class, better performance.
EN ISO 11612Protection against heat and flameLetter codes show which heat hazards were tested, such as flame spread and radiant heat.
EN ISO 14116Limited flame spreadShows the material will not keep burning once the flame is removed.
IEC 61482-2Thermal hazards of an electric arcThe arc rating tells you the energy the garment has been tested against.

Questions

Asked before booking

Do I really need a written risk assessment for a small salon or shop?

You must assess the risks whatever your size. In the UK the findings must be written down once you employ five or more people; in Ireland every employer needs a written assessment and safety statement. In practice councils, insurers and landlords ask to see a written record from much smaller businesses, so we always provide one.

Where do you travel?

All of the UK and Ireland. For work that needs travel and overnight stays we give you a quote that shows those costs separately.

Is the Construction Safety Pass the same as a Safe Pass or CSR card?

No. Our CSP is QTA's own accredited construction safety course. SOLAS Safe Pass in Ireland and the CSR card in Northern Ireland are separate schemes run by their own bodies, and each site decides which cards it accepts. Tell us the site and we will advise you honestly.

How long does a certificate last?

It depends on the course. Operator training is normally refreshed periodically and whenever the equipment or the job changes. We confirm the period for your course in writing.

Can you train on our own equipment?

Yes, and for forklifts, telehandlers, cranes and access platforms it is usually the best option, because the law expects training to be specific to the equipment people use.

Can you supply the PPE certificates?

Yes. Ask for the certificate and declaration of conformity for any garment before you buy.
